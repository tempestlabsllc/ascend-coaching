Chapter 2

02What skinny fat really is

Skinny fat means you look slim in clothes but soft without them: not much muscle, and a bit of fat sitting around your stomach and chest.

The problem isn’t too much fat. It’s too little muscle.

Most skinny fat people don’t actually carry much fat. They just don’t have enough muscle to give their body shape. That’s why the instinct to diet usually backfires.

If you cut first

You get smaller

You lose the little fat you have and some muscle with it. You end up thin, flat and still not happy. This is what happened to me.

If you build first

You get a frame

Muscle fills out your shoulders, chest, back and arms. Then when you lean out, there’s something to show.

The fix

Build, then reveal

Gain muscle with a controlled bulk, then recomp to strip the fat while keeping the muscle.

Signs you’re skinny fat

  • Normal or low bodyweight for your height, but a soft stomach and little muscle definition.
  • Thin arms, narrow shoulders and a flat chest, with fat around the belly, lower back or chest.
  • You’ve tried dieting and ended up looking smaller, not better.
  • Little or no consistent strength training so far.
Good news: skinny fat people are usually new to lifting, which means you’re in the best position you’ll ever be in to build muscle fast.
Chapter 3

03The roadmap

Three phases. Same direction I went, minus the mistake of cutting first.

Phase 1: Build the frame (lean bulk, about 4 to 8 months)Eat in a small surplus, train hard on a Push, Pull, Legs split, and gain weight slowly.
Phase 2: Get strong while you’re heavyThis runs inside Phase 1. Your focus is adding weight to the bar every week while the food is there to fuel it.
Phase 3: Reveal it (body recomposition)Drop to maintenance calories, keep protein high, and keep training heavy. The fat comes off and the muscle stays.

Where should you start?

What you see in the mirrorStart with
Thin arms and chest, a little softness around the belly, and you can see a faint outline of your abs in good lightingPhase 1: lean bulk
Soft all over, belly over your belt, no ab outline at all8 to 12 weeks of recomp at maintenance first, then Phase 1
You already have some muscle, but you’re not leanCut: eat about 300 to 500 below maintenance and keep lifting heavy, losing 0.5 to 1% of your bodyweight a week. Once you’re lean, go to Phase 1 to keep building
The one thing to avoid: cutting first when you don’t have much muscle yet. You’ll just end up skinny, like I did. Cutting only makes sense once you’ve built a frame worth revealing.

Chapter 5

05Eating enough when you’re a hard gainer

Gaining weight was hard for me too. I refused to get stuck, and I ate as much as I could. Here’s how to do the same thing, smarter.

Smarter than “eat everything”: research on lean versus big surpluses shows a bigger surplus mostly adds more fat, not more muscle. Stick to the calorie target from Chapter 4 and you’ll look a lot better at the end of your bulk.

Tricks that make it easier

  • Drink some calories. A shake with Fairlife milk, oats, a banana and peanut butter is 700+ calories and goes down easy.
  • Go calorie-dense. Rice, oats, bagels, pasta, granola, peanut butter, nuts, whole eggs and olive oil pack a lot of calories into a small amount of food.
  • Eat more often. 4 to 5 meals is easier than 3 huge ones. Add a meal before bed.
  • Don’t fill up on salad and water before meals. Eat your veggies, but not first.
  • Track it. Most hard gainers eat less than they think. Log your food for the first few weeks.

Brand new to lifting? Start with Push, Pull, Legs once a week (Mon, Wed, Fri) for your first 1 to 2 months, then move to 6 days.

Focus on getting stronger

While I was heavy, my focus was building strength and muscle, and that’s your job in Phase 1. Every exercise has a rep range. When you hit the top of the range on all 3 sets, add weight (about 5 lb upper body, 10 lb lower body). Write every session down and try to beat it next time.

  • Effort: finish each set with 1 to 2 good reps left in the tank.
  • Rest: 2 to 3 minutes on heavy lifts, 60 to 90 seconds on smaller ones.
  • Form first: controlled reps, full range of motion. Strength built with good form sticks.

Chapter 7

07The ugly middle of a bulk

Nobody talks about this part, so I will.

Edwin’s tip

Reminder: bulking makes you look worse. Your face gets fat and you look watery almost always. But looking back, I don’t regret it, because it allowed me to get to where I am now.

Why you look watery

  • Carbs hold water. Every gram of stored carbohydrate (glycogen) holds about 3 grams of water, so eating more carbs makes you look fuller and softer.
  • More food means more salt, which also holds water under your skin.
  • Some fat gain is part of the deal, and a lot of people notice it first in their face.

Most of the watery look disappears within a couple of weeks once you drop back to maintenance in Phase 3.

How to keep it under control

  • Stick to your surplus. Gaining faster than 0.5% a week mostly adds fat.
  • Drink plenty of water and keep salt fairly consistent day to day.
  • Keep your steps at 8,000 to 10,000 a day.
  • Judge progress by your lifts and your photos every 4 weeks, not by your face in the bathroom mirror.
Mindset: you’re building the frame. It’s supposed to look a little rough before it looks great.
Chapter 8

08Phase 3: Reveal it with a recomp

Lastly, I went into body recomposition to remove the fat and keep as much muscle as possible. This is where all the work from your bulk starts to show.

When to end your bulk

  • Your waist has gone up about 2 to 3 inches since you started.
  • You can no longer see any outline of your abs and you’re starting to feel too soft.
  • You’ve been bulking 4 to 8 months and your lifts have gone up a lot.

How to recomp

Drop to maintenanceTake away your surplus and eat at maintenance. If you have more fat to lose, go 200 to 300 below.
Keep protein high and keep training heavySame split, same effort. Heavy training is the signal that tells your body to hold on to the muscle you built.
Give it 12+ weeksJudge it by your waist, your photos and your lifts. The scale may barely move.

My Recomp Blueprint guide covers this phase step by step, with calorie charts, sample days and an adjustment table.

Then repeat. Most people need one or two rounds of build, then reveal, to go from skinny fat to an aesthetic physique. Each round, you start from a better place.
Chapter 9

09Mistakes and your checklist

Common skinny fat mistakes

  • Cutting first. You end up skinny instead of fit. (My biggest mistake.)
  • Dirty bulking. Eating everything in sight adds a lot more fat than muscle.
  • Doing endless cardio to fix the belly. Cardio burns calories, but it won’t build the muscle you’re missing.
  • Random workouts. No plan means no progressive overload. Follow the split and log it.
  • Low protein. Without 0.8 to 1 g per lb, your surplus turns into more fat.
  • Quitting in the ugly middle. Looking soft halfway through a bulk is normal. Stay the course.

Your weekly checklist

  • Hit my calorie target (maintenance + surplus) at least 6 days
  • Protein at 0.8 to 1 g per lb every day
  • All PPL sessions done and logged
  • Beat last week on at least one lift per workout
  • Weighed in daily and checked my weekly average (gaining 0.25 to 0.5% a week)
  • 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day and 7 to 9 hours of sleep
  • Progress photos and waist measurement every 4 weeks
